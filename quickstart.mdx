## What is this wiki?

The Liberal Wiki is a growing resource of libertarian and free market ideas, applied to real Indian policy questions. It is not a textbook, a manifesto, or a party platform. Think of it as a **living reference** — a place to find clear, honest answers to the kinds of questions that come up whenever someone defends markets, limited government, or individual freedom in an Indian context.

If someone asks you *"but who will build the roads?"* or *"won't free markets just exploit the poor?"*, this is the place to find a considered answer.

## Step 1: Find what you're looking for

The wiki is organised into **topic areas**, each covering a domain of Indian public policy.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion icon="wheat" title="Agriculture">
    Covers questions about farm laws, minimum support prices, subsidies, mandi regulations, and what free markets would actually mean for Indian farmers. Start with the [Agriculture FAQ](/faq).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="graduation-cap" title="School Education">
    Covers questions about government vs. private schools, the Right to Education Act, vouchers, teacher accountability, and why throwing more money at public schools hasn't worked. Start with the [Education FAQ](/faq).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="book" title="Glossary">
    New to free market economics? The [Glossary](/glossary) explains core concepts — from price signals to rent-seeking — in plain language without assuming prior knowledge.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Step 2: Understand how FAQ pages work

Each topic area has a main FAQ page that lists the most common objections and questions. These are written to be **honest about the strongest version of the opposing argument** before responding.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the question as a skeptic would ask it">
    Each FAQ entry starts with the question as a critic or skeptic would genuinely phrase it — not a straw man.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow the argument, not just the conclusion">
    The answers are meant to be reasoned through, not just asserted. If an answer doesn't convince you, the reasoning should at least be clear enough to disagree with specifically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the evidence">
    Where empirical claims are made, we try to link to research or data. If you spot a claim that isn't sourced, that's a contribution opportunity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 4: Contribute

This wiki is only as good as the people who build it. You don't need to be an economist or a policy expert to contribute — you need to care about getting things right.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion icon="lightbulb" title="Suggest a question">
    If you've encountered an objection to free markets that isn't covered here, open an issue on GitHub or reach out. The best FAQ entries come from real conversations.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="pen" title="Write an answer">
    If you know a topic well — agriculture, education, healthcare, labour law — you can draft a response. Read the [Contributing guide](/CONTRIBUTING) for style and sourcing standards.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="flag" title="Flag something wrong">
    If you think an answer is incorrect, incomplete, or unfair to the opposing view, flag it. We would rather be corrected than be confidently wrong.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
